# Geographic Concentration of Mandated CSR Spending in India (FY 2014–15 to FY 2023–24)

Data and code for an analysis of where India's mandatory corporate social responsibility (CSR) spending goes. Section 135 of the Companies Act 2013 requires large companies to spend 2% of their average net profits on CSR, but each company decides where. This repository measures how that spending is distributed across states and union territories, and whether it is lower where poverty is higher.

**Author:** Vaidanshi, Jayshree Periwal International School, Jaipur, India

## Questions

1. How concentrated is mandated CSR spending across states and union territories, in total and per resident?
2. Has that concentration changed as total spending grew?
3. Is spending per resident lower where multidimensional poverty is higher?
4. Do public sector undertakings (PSUs) and private companies differ, and is the pattern driven by a few large firms?

## Quick start

Requires Python 3.10 or later.

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python src/analysis.py   # prints all results and writes tables to results/
python src/figures.py    # writes the three figures to figures/
```

Run both commands from the repository root. The analysis takes under a minute.

## Method summary

- **Unit of observation:** one CSR project reported by one company in one financial year (628,701 records).
- **Cleaning:** state labels, PSU labels, and company names that differ only in capitalization or spacing are merged. For example, the raw data lists `Manipur` (₹208.28 crore) and `manipur` (₹2.64 crore) as separate states; grouping without this step understates Manipur's spending about 80-fold. After merging, the 47 raw state labels become 37 regions and 3 non-geographic categories.
- **Non-geographic spending:** "PAN India", "PAN India (Other Centralized Funds)", and "NEC/Not Mentioned" (together 33.7% of spending) are excluded from state-level measures and reported separately.
- **Per-resident spending:** ten-year state-assigned spending divided by 2011 Census population.
- **Inequality:** population-weighted Gini coefficient of per-resident spending, by year and for the decade.
- **Poverty test:** Spearman rank correlation between ten-year spending per resident and the NITI Aayog multidimensional poverty headcount ratio (2019–21) across 36 units, with a 5,000-resample bootstrap confidence interval.
- **Sensitivity checks:** states only; excluding Delhi; excluding units under one million residents; recent five years only; Kendall's τ; leave-one-out; PSU and non-PSU separately; excluding the ten largest spenders.

All amounts are in nominal Indian rupees (1 crore = 10 million rupees). No causal model is used.

## Key results

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Total reported spending | ₹2,20,601 crore |
| Not assigned to any state | 33.7% |
| Share of assigned spending to the top five regions (26.7% of population) | 55.4% |
| Gini coefficient of per-resident spending, FY 2014–15 → FY 2023–24 | 0.47 → 0.44 |
| Spending per resident, Delhi / Maharashtra / Bihar | ₹5,184 / ₹3,189 / ₹133 |
| Spearman ρ, spending per resident vs. poverty (n = 36) | −0.36 (p = 0.030; 95% CI −0.61 to −0.04) |
| Share of assigned spending to the nine highest-poverty units (42.4% of population) | 18.2% |
| Spearman ρ, private companies only | −0.41 (p = 0.013) |
| Spearman ρ, PSUs only | −0.06 (p = 0.71) |
| Spearman ρ, excluding the ten largest spenders | −0.41 (p = 0.014) |

Full output, including every sensitivity check, is in `results/results.txt`.

## Limitations

- Amounts are self-reported by companies, and the recorded state may be where a company or implementing organization is based rather than where beneficiaries live.
- One-third of spending cannot be assigned to a state, so regional totals are understated by an unknown amount.
- Populations are from the 2011 Census, the most recent complete census.
- The poverty comparison is ecological (state averages). It cannot show whether projects reached poor households within a state, and it does not establish causation.
